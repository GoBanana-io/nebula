## The problem

Olive trees cover the West Bank hillsides, but the families farming them had no way to reach foreign buyers: certification was out of reach for any single village, middlemen captured the margin, and some of the world's oldest groves sold their oil as an anonymous commodity.

## How it works

Founded by Jenin-raised anthropologist Nasser Abufarha, Canaan Palestine organised growers into the Palestine Fair Trade Association, built the systems for USDA Organic and fair-trade certification, and set up pressing and packing in Jenin. The company hand-picks indigenous landrace olives from 2,000+ family farms across 54 villages, cold-presses them at 28 degrees Celsius, and sells finished oil — the Jenin everyday bottle, the peppery Rumi, the delicate Nabali — alongside grains, spices, and preserves to mainstream distributors abroad.

## Pain points

Stranded harvests with no export paperwork, organic certification designed for estates rather than villages, commodity buyers indifferent to origin, and farming communities watching young people leave the land.

## Business model

Finished-goods margin: Canaan captures the bottling and branding premium that bulk oil never earns, sells wholesale and online, and routes fair-trade social premiums back to producer collectives for community investment.

## Challenges

Keeping certification audits clean across dozens of villages, moving harvests and finished goods through checkpoints and closures, and defending shelf space for a premium-priced oil against industrial Mediterranean brands.
