## The problem

Before Carousell, selling a used phone or sofa in Southeast Asia meant a desktop forum post, endless haggling over SMS, and meeting a stranger with cash. Listing was tedious, buyers flaked, and there was no protection for either side — so homes filled with idle goods while secondhand demand went unserved.

## How it works

Born at the 2012 NUS Startup Weekend hackathon as Team SnapSell, Carousell made listing as easy as snapping a photo. The app grew into a multi-category classifieds group — absorbing Mudah, Cho Tot and OneKyat through the 2019 merger with Telenor's 701Search — and then evolved into transaction-led recommerce: in-app payments and shipping, Certified inspection for luxury and phones, a Sell-to-Carousell instant-offer programme, and 29 physical stores by end-FY25.

## Pain points

Clunky incumbent forums built for desktops, fraud and fakes in high-value categories, flaky meetups with no payment rails, and dealers and consumers with no trusted channel between casual listings and full retail.

## Business model

Listings and advertising revenue from the classifieds base, take-rates and resale margins on managed recommerce, autos auction and property verticals, plus fintech referrals — a mix that made recommerce 45% of FY25 revenue, the group's largest contributor to growth.

## Challenges

The recommerce engine is operationally heavy — inspection, refurbishment and stores cost real money — while Shopee, Facebook Marketplace and traditional dealers fight for the same secondhand wallet. After a $1.1B valuation in 2021, investors expect sustained profitable growth, not just one EBITDA-positive year.

## Funding

- Raised: $56M from Naspers (2019, plus OLX Philippines acquisition), funding from Telenor with the 701Search merger (2019), $80M from Naver, Mirae Asset-Naver Asia Growth Fund and NH Investment & Securities (2020), and $100M from STIC Investments (2021) at a $1.1B valuation (NUS).
- Valuation: $1.1B at the STIC-led round (Sep 2021).

## Latest — Jul 2026

Carousell Group announced FY25 results: revenue up 18% year-on-year to $141M (nearly triple FY21), positive EBITDA for the first time on operating leverage and discipline, with recommerce revenue up 40% to 45% of the total — and set FY26 priorities of scaling recommerce while investing in AI for trust and discovery (company press release, 7 Jul 2026).
