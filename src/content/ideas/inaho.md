## The problem

Greenhouse tomato and asparagus growers race the ripening curve every day with fewer and older workers. Harvest is the most labor-intensive step, it happens in narrow windows, and in Japan fewer young workers will do night picking at any wage.

## How it works

Inaho's AI robot patrols the greenhouse at night, spots ripe snack tomatoes with vision models, and picks them while the grower sleeps; a sibling machine harvests asparagus. The robots run under a Robot-as-a-Service plan, and the company also co-develops cropping systems and farm-operation schemes with partners. Its approach won the Minister of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries Award at the 9th ROBOT AWARD.

## Pain points

Peak-season hands that never show up, wage bills that climb every year, and night-shift harvest work nobody wants.

## Business model

No robot sale: growers subscribe to harvesting-as-a-service with running costs the company pitches as comparable to manual labor, while Inaho keeps the hardware, software updates, and operations know-how.

## Challenges

The tomato robot still harvests only about 40% of matured fruit, with humans covering the rest by day; every new crop means training new vision models; and pushing into the Netherlands puts Inaho against the world's most advanced greenhouse-automation incumbents.
