## The problem

Google Play was not accessible to Iranian users, and even where reachable it offered no Persian catalogue and no way to pay with Iranian debit cards — so Iranian developers had no route to distribute apps, and users of hits like Clash of Clans resorted to insecure third-party sites for in-app purchases.

## How it works

Founded in April 2011 by Reza Mohammadi and Hessam Armandehi inside the Hezardastan group (which also operates the Divar classifieds platform), Cafe Bazaar is a Persian-first Android storefront with 500,000+ apps across applications, games and video. Users download free or paid apps and pay inside them with local debit cards; developers publish through Bazaar's console on a 70% revenue share, and local publishers localise foreign games for Persian-speaking buyers — only 28% of its 21,668 developer teams sit in Tehran.

## Pain points

No Google Play access, no Persian discovery, and no legitimate way to buy apps or in-game items without foreign gift cards or risky intermediaries.

## Business model

Marketplace take rate of roughly 30% on paid downloads and in-app purchases across tens of millions of installs, amplified by games merchandising and featuring.

## Challenges

Total dependence on one sanctioned market, policing malware and copycats across half a million listings, and keeping developer payouts viable through domestic payment rails as sanctions and currency swings bite.

## Funding

In 2018 Amsterdam-based International Internet Investment Coöperatief (IIIC) committed €38 million to parent Hezardastan for a 10% stake; no later rounds surfaced in inspected sources.

## Latest — April 2019

Cafe Bazaar announced it had surpassed 40 million users with roughly 20 million visits a week inside Iran — cementing a ~97% share of the domestic Android distribution market and its role as the default rail for Iranian app commerce.
