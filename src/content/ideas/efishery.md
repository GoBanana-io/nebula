## The problem

Feed is the dominant cost in fish farming, yet Indonesian smallholders fed ponds by hand with no measurement of appetite or water quality — wasting feed, polluting ponds, and inviting disease. Without records or collateral, the same farmers could not borrow formally and sold through layers of middlemen.

## How it works

Founded in Bandung in 2013 by Gibran Huzaifah with co-founder and CPO Chrisna Aditya, eFishery built IoT automatic feeders that dosed feed and monitored pond conditions, then expanded into an end-to-end platform: the eFarm and eFisheryKu monitoring apps, feed supply, loans through eFishery Fund, and market access for harvested fish and shrimp. The story carried it to a $90M Series C in 2022 and a $200M Series D in 2023 at a $1.4B valuation — Indonesia's first aquaculture unicorn, backed by SoftBank, Temasek, 42XFund and others.

## Pain points

Blind overfeeding and feed waste, late disease detection, zero access to bank credit, and buyers dictating farm-gate prices.

## Business model

Feeder hardware and software fees, feed-distribution margins, interest on farmer micro-loans, and take rates on fish traded through its marketplace.

## Challenges

The mission was cover for large-scale fraud: investigators found dual books since 2018, a network of founder-linked shell companies forging invoices and round-tripping funds, and 400,000 claimed feeders against ~24,000 real ones. Both co-founders were ousted in December 2024; the company fired 100 staff in January 2025 and ~300 more in February amid closure rumors; the ex-CEO admitted falsifying data by March 2025 with roughly $300M in investor funds unaccounted for, and police detained him and two former executives in August 2025. The company is defunct.

## Funding

- Raised: $90M Series C (2022, Temasek, SoftBank Vision Fund 2, Sequoia India) and a $200M Series D (2023) — over $294M from 28 institutional investors in total (Stratsea, TechCrunch via Wikipedia).
- Valuation: $1.4B at the 2023 Series D.

## Latest — May 2026

A May 2026 analysis of the case put the bottom line in investigative record: close to $600M of reported revenue did not exist, revenue was overstated roughly fivefold, and a reported profit masked a material loss — making eFishery Indonesia's largest startup fraud and a standing warning on private-market disclosure.
