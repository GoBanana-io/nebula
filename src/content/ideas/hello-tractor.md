## The problem

A smallholder farming two hectares needs a tractor for days, not a lifetime — yet buying one is out of reach, and Africa remains the least mechanised continent. Planting runs late, harvests shrink, and incomes stall while tractors that do exist sit idle for want of findable work.

## How it works

Hello Tractor started in Nigeria in 2014 as a marketplace where tractor owners lease idle machines to nearby smallholders. Booking agents aggregate scattered farmer requests into longer jobs, and IoT hardware plus mobile and web apps let owners locate, monitor, and manage each tractor — hours worked, fuel consumed, operator performance, maintenance flags. Its pay-as-you-go programme then finances tractors for high-performing agents, repaid from the leasing revenue itself rather than collateral.

## Pain points

Unmechanised plots and late planting, invisible idle tractor capacity, and creditworthy agripreneurs labelled unbankable for lack of traditional collateral.

## Business model

Software and marketplace fees around tractor bookings and fleet management, plus the financing book: tractors funded through pay-as-you-go loans that had disbursed $4 million by August 2022, targeting $15 million by year-end.

## Challenges

Field hardware must survive dust, heat, and patchy connectivity; the loan book depends on harvests and repayments holding up; and scaling across countries means retraining agents and owners market by market.

## Funding

- Raised: $1 million from Heifer International (May 2022) for pay-as-you-go tractor loans through a programme active in Nasarawa, Abuja, and Enugu.
- Strategic: John Deere invested via its Startup Collaborator programme, tying Hello Tractor into its small-equipment connectivity ambitions for Africa and Asia.
- Valuation: MISSING.

## Latest — August 2022

In August 2022 John Deere's investment in Hello Tractor was reported alongside the Startup Collaborator partnership: the companies aim to connect small farmers with affordable mechanisation while helping contractors run more efficiently, with Hello Tractor reporting 3,000 fleet owners and around half a million farmers served.
