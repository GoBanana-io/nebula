## The problem

When BeiGene was founded in Beijing in 2010, China could make generic drugs but had no company capable of inventing a first-class cancer therapy and proving it in global trials — while blood-cancer patients everywhere needed better-tolerated targeted options than chemotherapy and early BTK inhibitors offered.

## How it works

BeOne discovers targeted oncology drugs in its own labs, led by Brukinsa (zanubrutinib), a BTK inhibitor for B-cell malignancies, alongside the PD-1 inhibitor Tevimbra. It runs global clinical trials across dozens of countries, manufactures at its own biologics facilities, and sells through commercial organisations in the US — its largest market — Europe and China. In May 2025 the company completed a redomiciliation from the Cayman Islands to Switzerland and changed its name from BeiGene Ltd. to BeOne Medicines Ltd., trading in New York under ONC alongside its Hong Kong and Shanghai listings.

## Pain points

Harsh chemotherapy regimens, resistance and relapse in B-cell cancers, tolerability limits of earlier targeted drugs, and Chinese patients' dependence on expensive imported innovative medicines.

## Business model

Approved-drug product sales, overwhelmingly Brukinsa, concentrated in the US with growing Europe and rest-of-world contribution, plus partnership and co-development income.

## Challenges

BeOne spent years loss-making while funding global trials; it now fights entrenched Western oncology giants indication by indication, and must justify a complex triple listing and Swiss redomiciliation to investors watching margins.
