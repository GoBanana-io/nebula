## The problem

The alt-meat aisle conquered the burger and then stalled: shoppers consistently name texture and taste as the reasons they stop buying, and nobody has cracked an affordable whole marbled steak. Extruded pea protein reads as mince, not muscle — so the premium center-of-plate occasion stays firmly animal.

## How it works

Chef Bakrin Ahn's Millennial Dining in Seocho has served vegan Korean classics since 2020, and its spin-off Millennial Flavor Town bottles that kitchen R&D into a five-step patented fermentation: soybeans, mushrooms, red beet powder, rice koji, and oils ferment into a high-protein, cholesterol-free base. Shredded beef goes into ready-to-eat jars in soy-sauce and truffle flavours, while the Millennial Marbles whole cut fakes Wagyu-style marbling with beet powder and coconut oil, finished with charcoal-grill aroma to win over meat-eaters.

## Pain points

Rubbery, beany, or mushy textures that turn first-time buyers into never-again buyers; Korean vegan kitchens importing their premium analogues; and a category where every failed tasting poisons the whole shelf.

## Business model

No public pricing yet — the concept earns today through the restaurant and jar sales, with the plan to wholesale whole cuts to restaurants and retailers as it enters the US and Europe, starting from its London expo showcase.

## Challenges

A paper-stage exporter: no disclosed funding or factory, novel-food and labelling gauntlets in each new market, and entrenched whole-cut competitors — Redefine Meat, Slovenia's Juicy Marbles, America's Chunk Foods — already on European shelves with far more capital.

## Latest — November 2024

Millennial Flavor Town unveiled its marbled steak and shredded beef at the Plant Based World Europe expo in London (November 13–14), weeks after winning a $6,000 award at the Korea-ASEAN Business Model Competition for Sustainable Development Goals in Indonesia — the furthest the concept has publicly travelled toward its US and Europe launch.
