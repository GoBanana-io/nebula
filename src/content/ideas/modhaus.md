## The problem

K-pop runs on superfans who stream, bulk-buy albums, and organize global voting campaigns — yet agencies decide everything and fans decide nothing. Engagement peaks around comebacks and then the monetization stops.

## How it works

Modhaus is an entertainment company behind tripleS, ARTMS, and idntt, paired with the Cosmo fan app. Fans collect digital photocards that double as voting tokens on real group decisions (units, concepts), finish interactive quests, and follow member content — with blockchain kept invisible through mainstream onboarding. In July 2025 Cosmo launched on the Abstract blockchain for performance and scale.

## Pain points

Zero fan influence despite massive fan spending, dead revenue periods between comebacks, and Web3 onboarding too complex for the teenage mainstream audience.

## Business model

Group revenue (music, concerts, physical merch) plus ongoing Cosmo collectible and vote-token sales — every comeback and every fan vote becomes a transaction.

## Challenges

Crypto mechanics risk regulatory attention and fan backlash; the participatory model must replicate tripleS's success across newer groups; and incumbents like HYBE's Weverse could add voting features without any blockchain.

## Latest — July 2025

Modhaus partnered with the Abstract blockchain and launched Cosmo on it — email/passkey onboarding, collectible photocards, fan voting, and quests running on invisible rails — while tripleS counted 3.4M followers after winning Best New Female Artist at the 2023 MAMA Awards (PR Newswire).
