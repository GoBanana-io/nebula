## The problem

Standardized-test prep treats every student identically: the same question banks, the same lectures, and tutors who can only guess where each learner stands. Students burn months — and families burn hagwon fees — drilling material they may already know, with no reliable signal of what their score will be until exam day.

## How it works

Riiid applies knowledge tracing at scale. After a short diagnostic, its models predict the student's likely score, pinpoint the concepts dragging it down, and continuously serve the questions with the highest expected score gain. The engine powers Riiid's own Santa apps — Santa TOEIC plus GMAT prep with Kaplan, Santa Realtor for real-estate exams, and an insurance-agent exam tool — and is licensed into partners' apps: ACT prep with ConnecME across MENA, and college-entrance prep with Grupo INICIE (Brazil's ENEM) and Casa Grande (Colombia's SABER 11) under $3M+ contracts.

## Pain points

Blind drilling with no feedback loop, high private-tutoring costs, and exam-day surprises — students discover too late that their preparation never targeted their actual weaknesses.

## Business model

Two tracks: consumer subscriptions on the Santa app family, and B2B platform licensing plus co-development contracts with publishers and education firms worldwide. Underpinning both is an IP moat — 103 domestic and international patent applications, 27 issued at the time of the SoftBank round.

## Challenges

The 2021-era edtech boom deflated, making fresh capital scarce; every new exam or language demands new content partners and calibration data before the engine works; and free AI chatbots now tutor students for nothing, pressuring both the consumer and licensing businesses to prove measurable score lift.
