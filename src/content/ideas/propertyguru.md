## The problem

Finding a home in Southeast Asia used to mean scanning newspaper classifieds or walking past agent windows. Listings were scattered, prices were hearsay, and neither seekers nor agents had data — a high-stakes market running on anecdote.

## How it works

Founded in Singapore in 2007 by Steve Melhuish and Jani Rautiainen, PropertyGuru aggregated listings country by country until it owned the top of the property-search funnel. The marketplace now connects over 31 million seekers with more than 50,000 agents each month across four markets, wrapped with price insights, PropertyGuru Finance mortgage matching, and agent-enterprise software.

## Pain points

No single inventory of available homes, no trusted price history for buyers and renters, mortgage shopping done branch by branch, and agents invisible online without their own marketing.

## Business model

Recurring subscriptions from agents and developers, pay-to-promote listings and leads, referral fees from home loans, and enterprise data products — marketplace toll-taking on the region's largest consumer purchase.

## Challenges

Listings businesses ride property cycles and government cooling measures; PropertyGuru never took a cut of transactions themselves, capping monetization; its NYSE chapter (via SPAC) ended with a take-private at $6.70 a share; and EQT's first act was replacing the CEO and chairman — private ownership brings patience, but also a demand for faster execution.

## Funding

- Raised: backed for seventeen years by shareholders led by TPG and KKR (EQT, Dec 2024); earlier round details unverified — omitted.
- Valuation: ~$1.1B equity value at $6.70 per share in the EQT/BPEA Fund VIII acquisition (Dec 2024).

## Latest — Jan 2025

One month after EQT completed the $1.1B acquisition and delisted PropertyGuru from the NYSE (shares ceased trading 13 Dec 2024), the company announced new leadership with incoming CEO Lewis Ng and a new chairman — EQT's reset for the private chapter (Mingtiandi, 13 Jan 2025).
