## The problem

Commercial farms managing thousands of acres irrigate, fertilize and spray largely on schedule and instinct. Water is wasted, inputs are over-applied, disease is spotted late, and nobody can forecast the harvest accurately enough to plan around it.

## How it works

Founded by Omar Elboghdady (CEO) with Marwan Eltoukhy and Mohamed Elsawy, Viridia Tech fuses satellite imagery and meteorological data with context-sensitive AI models. The platform pushes near-real-time, customized recommendations to growers — when to irrigate, where disease risk is rising, how the yield is shaping up — starting with potato and olive crops where the six-person team built its first edge.

## Pain points

Resource efficiency for water, fertilizer and pesticides; early detection of operational issues such as heightened disease risk; and yield forecasting for effective resource planning — the three gaps Elboghdady names for commercial producers.

## Business model

A B2B analytics platform sold to industrial agricultural companies, with the pitch anchored on double-digit gains in yield, unit economics and sustainability metrics. Pricing and contract terms are undisclosed; the company was still bootstrapped as of September 2024.

## Challenges

This is a paper-stage idea: no shipped scale, no paying-customer base on record. The founders name trust as the hardest wall — convincing farmers and agronomists to follow model recommendations that contradict practices unchanged by recent climate shifts — alongside diversifying crops, deepening the models, and closing a first seed round.

## Funding

- Raised: none — bootstrapped to a six-person team as of September 2024; the November 2024 Mega Green Accelerator win brought a $100,000 grand prize, not an equity round. A seed fundraising round was planned but no raise is on record.
- Valuation: none.

## Latest — November 2024

At the Mega Green Accelerator Demo Day in Riyadh, Viridia Tech was named overall winner of the 2024 edition — run by PepsiCo, SABIC, AstroLabs and partners across a six-month programme — taking the $100,000 grand prize for its AI and data-analytics platform for farmers.
