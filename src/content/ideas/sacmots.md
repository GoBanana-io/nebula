## The problem

A single degree of warming shaves 5–10% off wheat, soy, and corn yields, and coffee and olive oil prices have already multiplied — while the fixes on offer (gene editing, heavy inputs) stall on cost or public trust.

## How it works

Spun out of Kyushu University's century-old crop-science laboratory, SACMOTs applies controlled environmental stress to parent plants to lift the epigenetic locks on dormant genes — two to three thousand shifting at once in rice — so offspring inherit whole-plant resilience with the DNA sequence untouched. Its banner is "Green Revolution 2.0": upgrading seeds, fertilizer, and agrochemicals together.

## Pain points

Heat-driven yield losses, input bills farmers can't pass on, and a market that resists gene-edited seed even where regulators allow it.

## Business model

Near-term paid PoCs from the Fukuoka FiaS/Ito Lab Plus base; the long-term bet is treated seed sold each season — if the team proves the effect holds stably into grandchild generations.

## Challenges

A months-old company (incorporated December 2025) with lab-scale proof: inheritance duration unproven, the seed-pricing model unresolved, and no disclosed venture funding — survival depends on converting PoCs into a seed round.
