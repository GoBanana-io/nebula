## The problem

Molecular testing across Africa leans on imported kits that are expensive, need cold chains, and are rarely designed around the continent's highest-burden diseases — so patients go undiagnosed or pay out of reach.

## Why it worked

Founded in 2023 by Salma Tammam, Reme-D develops and manufactures its own molecular diagnostic tests in Egypt, engineered to be affordable and temperature-resilient for underserved markets. Local production plus a focus on high-burden diseases (TB, HIV, hepatitis, HPV) won backing from impact and health investors betting that domestic manufacturing can close Africa's testing gap.

## How it works

Reme-D designs molecular assays from scratch for the conditions they will actually face — heat, patchy cold chains, low-infrastructure labs — then manufactures them at its Cairo facility. The kits are room-temperature stable, priced up to six times below imported alternatives, and already cover TB, HIV, hepatitis and HPV, with genetic-disease and cancer panels in development. Hospitals and labs buy the kits directly; 550,000+ tests have shipped across Egypt, Kenya and Sudan.

## Pain points

African labs depend on imported kits built for Western cold-chain settings: expensive, temperature-fragile, and seldom prioritising the continent's highest-burden diseases. The result is missed diagnoses and testing priced out of reach.

## Business model

Straightforward B2B manufacturing: develop once, produce locally at low cost, and sell kits to hospitals and labs country by country — with each new assay and each new market compounding the catalogue.

## Challenges

Each new assay and each new country means fresh regulatory clearance; scaling manufacturing without quality slips is capital-intensive; and global diagnostics incumbents contest every tender.
