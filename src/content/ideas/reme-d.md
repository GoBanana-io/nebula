## The idea

Molecular testing across Africa leans on imported kits that are expensive, need cold chains, and are rarely designed around the continent's highest-burden diseases — so patients go undiagnosed or pay out of reach. Reme-D flips it: develop and manufacture the tests in Egypt, engineered for heat, price, and the diseases that actually burden the region.

## How it works

- Designs its own PCR-style assays for TB, HIV, hepatitis, and HPV.
- Manufactures locally, cutting import cost and lead time.
- Engineers temperature resilience so kits survive real-world supply chains.
- Expands the menu (genetic-disease and cancer panels) on the same base.

## Who pays

Per-kit buyers (labs, hospitals, programs) plus tender and distributor volume. Each cleared assay is a new SKU on sunk manufacturing.

## Why it worked

Proximity is the product: local manufacturing plus burden-first design beats a superior foreign kit that melts, delays, or prices out.

## Challenges

Each new assay and each new country means fresh regulatory clearance; scaling manufacturing without quality slips is capital-intensive; global diagnostics incumbents contest every tender.

## Steal this

In any import-dependent health market, start from the disease burden data, not the catalog: design for heat, power cuts, and price first — then manufacture close to the patient.
