## The problem

A bank, hospital network, or government ministry wants generative AI over its own documents — but compliance forbids sending customer records to a foreign consumer chatbot, and frontier models demand GPU budgets only hyperscalers can afford. Generic AI either fails the security review or fails the finance review.

## How it works

Cohere trains the Command family of LLMs — deliberately efficiency-tuned so they run on limited GPUs — plus an Embed/Rerank stack for retrieval-augmented generation over enterprise data. Customers call the models via API or run them privately inside their own VPC, on-prem data centre, or sovereign cloud. North, launched in summer 2025, sits on top: a workspace where business teams build custom AI agents and document workflows without data ever leaving the enterprise boundary. CEO Aidan Gomez co-authored Google's "Attention Is All You Need" transformer paper before founding the company in 2019 with Ivan Zhang and Nick Frosst.

## Pain points

Data-residency laws and procurement rules that disqualify public AI clouds; GPU scarcity and inference bills; hallucinating chatbots that cannot cite company sources.

## Business model

Enterprise platform subscriptions plus usage-based API pricing, weighted toward multi-year private-deployment contracts — the segment that drove the jump from $35M annualized revenue in March 2024 to $240M ARR in 2025.

## Challenges

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are all chasing the same enterprise budgets with far larger balance sheets; the $5.5B valuation needs continued 50%-plus quarterly growth to look cheap; and every private deployment is operationally heavier than a single public cloud.

## Funding

- Raised: $500M Series D (Jul 2024) led by PSP Investments with Cisco, Fujitsu, AMD, and Export Development Canada (Nvidia and Salesforce also back the company).
- Valuation: $5.5B at the July 2024 round.

## Latest — February 2026

Cohere told investors it beat its $200M ARR target for 2025, closing the year at $240M with quarter-over-quarter growth above 50% all year, and CEO Aidan Gomez has said an IPO could come "soon" — potentially pitting a Toronto listing against the big US AI debuts expected in 2026.
