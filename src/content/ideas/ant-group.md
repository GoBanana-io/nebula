## The problem

As Alibaba's marketplaces exploded in the 2000s, payments were the missing layer: strangers would not trust each other enough to transact, and hundreds of millions of ordinary Chinese had no credit cards and no access to basic wealth or credit products.

## How it works

Alipay started in 2004 as escrow inside Alibaba checkouts — buyer money held until goods arrived — then spread to QR-code payments at every street stall in China. Ant Financial was spun off in October 2014 (renamed Ant Group in 2020) and stacked financial products inside the wallet: Yu'e Bao money-market savings, Huabei consumer credit, Ant Insurance, plus Ant International for cross-border payments and Ant Digital Technologies for blockchain. Alibaba holds roughly a third of Ant and books its share of profit a quarter in arrears.

## Pain points

Zero trust between unknown buyers and sellers; cash-only daily life with no transaction records; savings earning nothing and credit available only to the well-banked; merchants drowning in cash handling.

## Business model

Take a small cut of enormous payments volume, then monetise the user base with credit interest, fund-management fees, and insurance distribution — and now pour the cash cow into AI: a record $5.17 billion of AI R&D in 2025, an AQ AI health app, and pivots toward agentic commerce and embodied-AI services.

## Challenges

Regulators suspended the $34.5 billion dual IPO days before launch in October 2020 — then valued at $313 billion — and forced Ant into a PBOC-supervised financial holding company, permanently constraining the lending flywheel. Profits swing wildly with investment cycles (down 79% one quarter, up 1% the next), and the AI/health pivot has yet to prove it can replace payments growth.
