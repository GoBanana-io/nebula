## The problem

Before Sociolla, buying cosmetics in Indonesia meant gambling: markets and generic online sellers mixed counterfeits with originals, genuine imported brands had barely any organised distribution, and shoppers outside Jakarta malls simply could not find them.

## How it works

Founded in 2015 by Chrisanti Indiana, Christopher Madiam and John Rasjid, Social Bella started Sociolla as a beauty-only e-commerce portal guaranteeing authentic products. It grew into an ecosystem: the Sociolla shop, the Soco review community where users share experiences and videos, the Beauty Journal publication, and O2O concept stores. By 2018 the three online properties had logged 20.2 million visitors, and the company kept expanding the store footprint at home while entering Vietnam.

## Pain points

Fake cosmetics of unknown safety, no trustworthy shade or skin-type guidance, and authentic brands unreachable beyond big-city malls.

## Business model

Retail margin on thousands of curated SKUs plus exclusive brand distribution, in-store beauty services, and marketing services that sell brands access to Sociolla's audience and O2O funnel.

## Challenges

Dozens of physical stores tie up capital in rent and inventory; Sephora and the Shopee/Tokopedia beauty aisles compete brutally on price and assortment; and each new market needs its own brand mix and community flywheel.

L Catterton announced a strategic investment in Social Bella International — its first-ever investment in Indonesia — joining the board to back Sociolla's push to new Gen-Z and Millennial consumers in Indonesia and Vietnam across its Sociolla, Soco, Beauty Journal, Lilla and SDI platforms.
