## The problem

Saudi government services ran on paper, queues and in-person procedures across security, health, transport and finance. The state needed a trusted national builder that could digitise registries and citizen-facing services at population scale without compromising security.

## How it works

Elm builds and runs the digital infrastructure behind government platforms — digital identity, e-government portals, AI and data analytics, smart cities, healthcare and digital banking systems — and operates them (2B+ transactions a year). Through seven subsidiaries it adds consultation, data analysis, business-process outsourcing and HR services, and it now exports the model regionally with compliance platforms in Oman, archiving and ERP in Jordan, and judicial digitisation in Iraq.

## Pain points

Fragmented agency systems, paper bureaucracy, and the extreme security and reliability bar for national registries and citizen data — plus the need to keep 170+ large projects running while adding AI capabilities.

## Business model

Primarily fees from digital services to public- and private-sector clients, layered with outsourcing contracts, customised IT solutions and professional services. It has also deployed USD60M+ across 20 company investments and USD33M+ across eight acquisitions.

## Challenges

Growth still leans on government demand; regional engagements must convert into durable revenue; and with no clean listed peer on Tadawul, the stock trades at a premium (~24x trailing earnings per one valuation study) that leaves little room for execution slips.

## Funding

- Listed on the Saudi Exchange (Tadawul: 7203) on 16 February 2022 at SAR 128 per share (top of the SAR 113–128 range), valuing the company at about SAR 10.24B at offer; the 24M shares sold came entirely from the selling shareholder, PIF, which retained ~70%.
- Valuation: SAR 10.24B at IPO offer (Feb 2022, IPOX).

## Latest — July 2026

On 3 July 2026 Elm announced it will showcase the Saudi digital-transformation model at LEAP East Hong Kong (8–10 July), its first outing beyond Riyadh, pitching three decades of government partnership — 30M+ users, 170+ projects, 2B+ annual transactions — to APAC technology partners and investors.
