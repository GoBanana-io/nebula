## The problem

Gaza families pay punishing prices to heat and cook — a gas cylinder costs 64 shekels ($18.47) — while olive presses dump tens of thousands of tonnes of jift each year into sewers and fields, where its polyphenols poison crops and seep into aquifers. Meanwhile nearly 80% of young university graduates cannot find work.

## How it works

Civil engineering graduates Tamer Abo Motlaq, Usama Qudaih, and Khaled Abo Motlaq grind fresh olive-mill pomace, treat it to remove the bad smell and cut smoke, compress it through a pellet machine into cylindrical blocks with air gaps, and sun-dry the result. Unable to afford the $11,000 commercial machine, they built their own from scratch in a local workshop for $3,000. The pellets fuel home heating and cooking, and Khan Younis farmers burn them for up to 12 hours of greenhouse and chicken-farm heat.

## Pain points

Unaffordable cooking gas, unsustainable logging of scarce trees for firewood, toxic olive waste with nowhere to go, and educated youth with no employers.

## Business model

Volume sales of a cheaper fuel: at $0.20/kg — about half the firewood price — a few cents' worth of jift substitutes for an $18.47 gas cylinder, with factories seeking year-round supply contracts and a portable phone-charging heater on the drawing board.

## Challenges

A single self-built machine caps output at 1,000kg an hour against demand that already exceeds supply; pomace arrives seasonally with the harvest; and beyond the $5,000 micro-grant there is no capital cushion for a second machine or a larger workforce.

## Funding

- Raised: $5,000 micro-funding plus technical assistance and coaching from the Ma'an Development Center, won through a Danish Church Aid startup contest (Al Jazeera, Dec 2019).

## Latest — Dec 2019

In a 17 December 2019 Al Jazeera feature, the founders reported demand outrunning their 1,000kg-an-hour output, factory buyers requesting year-round contracts, and plans to double capacity and staff within a year alongside the portable-heater prototype.
