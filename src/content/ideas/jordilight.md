## The problem

A night-shift technician or a hiker after dark carries essentially 20th-century gear: a light that is on or off, with no way to signal a fall, adjust to the task, or stay connected once the phone loses signal.

## How it works

JordiLight's concept, as listed on Jordan's national StartupsJo directory, rebuilds outdoor and work gear around smart lighting: Bluetooth control, fall sensors, and customizable light output, expanding toward GPS- and LTE-enabled devices for off-grid visibility.

## Pain points

Dumb single-mode lights, no automatic fall alert for lone workers, no remote customization, and accessories from generic brands with no service or roadmap behind them.

## Business model

Sell the smart gear itself; the planned GPS/LTE line opens a second act of connected-device revenue if the company ever ships it at scale.

## Challenges

This is a paper-stage idea: no founders, funding rounds, or device shipments are publicly documented — only accelerator selections and the company's own StartupsJo claims ($130K revenue, $1.3M pipeline, both self-reported and unverified). Hardware unit economics, manufacturing partners, and retail distribution are all unproven, and deep-pocketed lighting brands can add Bluetooth faster than a startup can tool a factory.

## Funding

- Raised: none publicly disclosed — no funding rounds reported for JordiLight. Oasis500's June 2020 cohort announcement mentioned investments of up to $100,000 per company, with no per-company closing disclosed.
- Valuation: none.

## Latest — September 2023

In September 2023 Flat6Labs named JordiLight among the 24 Jordan-, Lebanon-, and Iraq-founded startups in its StartMashreq Growth Track program, a June 2023 to December 2024 track backing companies with a combined $20.8M in annual recurring revenue and $38.5M previously raised.
