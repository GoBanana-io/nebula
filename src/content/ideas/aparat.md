## The problem

Global video platforms are filtered in Iran, so Persian-speaking creators and viewers had no fast, local home for uploading and watching online video — YouTube exists only behind circumvention tools while domestic bandwidth favours home-grown services.

## How it works

Launched in 2011 by Tehran's Saba Idea (Android app February 2011), Aparat is a free user-generated video site: anyone can upload clips into Persian-language categories, creators may put selected videos behind paywalls, and official approved channels host political, cultural and brand accounts. It scaled through national moments — World Cup broadcasts, the "Iranian Prodigies" talent contest and Aparat Kids in 2014 — and later spun off Aparat Sport, Aparat Style and the Filimo streaming service.

## Pain points

Slow or blocked access to global video sites, no Persian-first discovery, and no local channel infrastructure for Iranian public figures, educators and advertisers.

## Business model

Advertising-led: SabaVision, Saba Idea's online ad agency, manages on-platform advertising, while paid channels let uploaders charge for premium videos.

## Challenges

Running 40 Gbit/s of video bandwidth with thousands of daily uploads under sanctions, moderating content to regulators' satisfaction without alienating creators, and relying on the domestic ad market for effectively all revenue.

## Funding

No verifiable funding rounds found in inspected sources; Aparat grew inside the privately held Saba Idea group on advertising revenue.

## Latest — November 2021

A Washington Institute analysis of Iran's internet landscape named Aparat alongside domestic app-store clones as the established local substitutes Iranians would fall back on, as the conservative administration threatened to restrict Instagram — confirmation of Aparat's status as Iran's equivalent to YouTube.
