## The problem

Tesla's Optimus and its rivals aim at factories first. Founder Shuo Yang's starting point was personal: after tuning Optimus algorithms late into the night while his toddler son slept, he promised the boy a robot of his own — "like Nick", the companion from his bedtime stories — and realised no such product existed.

## How it works

Mondo Robotics is developing small household robots designed as companions: compact, user-friendly and safe, running modern ML on an embedded and mechatronic stack the team builds itself rather than buying off the shelf. The founding thesis, published by Yang in April 2025, is that smaller, accessible consumer robots can be both meaningful products today and foundational platforms for the future.

## Pain points

Parents want trustworthy, screen-free companionship for children; existing robots are either industrial machines or passive toys; and nothing on the market combines mobility, intelligence and child-safe design at a household price.

## Business model

Consumer robot sales first — a companion product households buy the way they buy high-end appliances — with a self-owned software stack that can later support services, content and developer-built behaviours.

## Challenges

Everything is still to prove: no product specifications have been revealed, no funding disclosed, and the team must turn a father's hypothesis into a manufacturable, certifiable, desirable product while incumbents race toward the same living room.
