## The problem

Syria's young people were stuck between two broken options: formal education that taught theory employers didn't need, and online courses that offered certificates with no practice and no local path into a job — all while the labor market kept shifting under a war-damaged economy.

## How it works

Doroob is a Syrian startup built around learning for employment rather than learning for credentials. Learners move between flexible online lessons, in-person sessions, and blended tracks delivered through a digital platform wired to a growing network of physical training centers. In June 2026 Doroob announced a strategic partnership with eYouth, one of the region's leading EdTech platforms — combining eYouth's extensive digital library and trainer-mentor network with Doroob's on-the-ground centers to develop and expand employment-focused programs for Syrian youth.

## Pain points

Skill mismatches between graduates and employers, purely online providers with no hands-on component, and a generation trying to enter the workforce without guidance on what the market actually wants.

## Business model

Enrollment-driven training programs, amplified by the eYouth alliance: a bigger content catalogue, experienced mentors, and an employer-facing pipeline that turns completions into job placements — eYouth's network has already helped 700,000 graduates access opportunities across 21 countries (entArabi, Jun 2026).

## Challenges

Training centers depend on physical infrastructure in a country rebuilding it; connectivity is uneven; and the hardest metric — graduates actually hired — has to be proven cohort after cohort in an unstable labor market.
