## The idea

Like MaxAB, Capiter targeted the fragmented restocking chain feeding Egypt's mom-and-pop retailers — ordering, delivery, and embedded credit for small shops. The thesis was validated by surviving peers. The company wasn't.

## How it worked (while it ran)

- One ordering app replaced layers of middlemen for small shops.
- Direct sourcing plus route delivery compressed cost.
- Order history was meant to underwrite shop credit — the compounding prize.

## Why it died

Capiter raised a $33M Series A in September 2021 and burned through it in under a year. In September 2022 the board ousted founders Mahmoud and Ahmed Noah, alleging mismanagement and failure to engage with the board; TechCrunch reported the company in arrears to employees and creditors. The entity effectively ceased trading and was liquidated — a textbook governance failure, not a market failure.

## Challenges

Founder–board trust collapsed completely; no bridge financing materialized once governance blew up; staff and suppliers were left unpaid, freezing any acqui-hire or asset-sale recovery.

## Steal this (the lesson)

- Prove one city's routes profitable before buying the country's map.
- Put cash controls and board reporting in place at the seed stage, not after the Series A lands.
- Treat credit as a second business with its own underwriting — not as a growth subsidy for the first.
