## The problem

Iran has no privately owned television networks and global streamers stay out under sanctions, so Iranians had no legal, Persian-first subscription home for films and series — only state TV, pirated copies or satellite dishes.

## How it works

Begun in February 2015 as "Filimo Aparat" inside the Saba Idea group and soon rebranded to Filimo, the service sells monthly subscriptions for unlimited Iranian and international films, series and documentaries across Android, iOS, web and Android TV apps. Growth was explosive — over a billion minutes viewed in 2016 — and the company extended to the living room with the Filimo Box streaming device in August 2019, free holiday viewing windows as a funnel, and Televika, a sister service exporting West Asian cinema to viewers abroad.

## Pain points

No private channels, global streamers unavailable, and fragmented piracy or satellite sources with no dependable Persian catalogue, subtitles or streaming quality.

## Business model

Subscription video on demand: recurring monthly fees from more than 400,000 subscribers, topped up with advertising and Filimo Box hardware sales.

## Challenges

Full content supervision handed to the state broadcaster IRIB, the cost of licensing and producing originals on a domestic budget, and piracy plus satellite TV chasing the same audience.

## Funding

No verifiable funding rounds found in inspected sources; Filimo grew inside the privately held Saba Idea group on subscription revenue.

## Latest — June 2023

Iran's Supreme Cultural Revolution Council gave the state broadcaster IRIB full control over privately owned streaming services at a June 20 meeting — months after IRIB's chief demanded Filimo be blocked over the series Collapse (Fall) — putting the country's most popular VOD platform under direct state-programming supervision.
