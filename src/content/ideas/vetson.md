## The problem

Large-animal vets are scarce across rural Canada: a farmer can face a 45-minute to three-hour drive for a simple call, while stretched practices cannot cover remote clients — a gap that threatens herd health and farm livelihoods.

## How it works

Vet clinics join the VETSon service and give their farm clients a login. Farmers open the app to book telemedicine visits and virtual locum cover with their practice, and an e-commerce module sends prescriptions to distributors that ship animal medicines straight to the farm — with traceable records for regulators.

## Pain points

No vet available for livestock emergencies, long drives for routine calls, and the cumbersome routine of collecting every prescribed medicine in person from the clinic.

## Business model

VETSon sells through the practices: clinics join the platform and onboard their own farm clients, and the e-commerce prescribing flow (currently Ontario-only, given province-by-province veterinary regulation) takes inventory and shipping work off clinic staff. Pricing is undisclosed.

## Challenges

Each province regulates veterinarians separately, so direct-to-farm medicine shipping works only in Ontario for now; scaling means winning over regulators province by province. The company runs on grant funding (OAFRI) plus early clinic adoption — institutional capital and a proven revenue engine are still ahead of it.
