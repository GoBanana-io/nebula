## The problem

India's electricity demand keeps climbing while coal dominates supply. Utilities needed gigawatt-scale clean power with credible developers behind it, and corporates chasing net-zero targets needed large, bankable renewable contracts — plus a domestic alternative to imported solar panels.

## How it works

Founded in 2011 by Sumant Sinha and headquartered in Gurugram, ReNew develops, builds, owns, and operates utility-scale wind, solar, hydro, hybrid, and battery-storage projects — 150+ sites across 10 states with about 4,500 employees. It sells power through long-term agreements to state utilities and to corporate customers including Microsoft, Amazon, and Google; its ReNew Green arm handles commercial-and-industrial supply; and its factories manufacture solar modules and cells. In 2021 it became the first Indian renewable-energy company listed on Nasdaq via a SPAC merger.

## Pain points

Utilities short on firm clean supply, corporates needing verifiable green power for net-zero pledges, and a national dependence on imported modules that domestic manufacturing can displace.

## Business model

Recurring revenue from long-term power contracts on owned assets, corporate clean-energy solutions and carbon-credit services, and manufacturing income from 6.5 GW of module and 2.5 GW of cell capacity.

## Challenges

Auction tariffs keep compressing returns, land acquisition and grid hookups slow new sites, and funding generation, storage, and factory expansion at once keeps balance-sheet pressure high.

## Funding

- Valuation: MISSING.

## Latest — Apr 2026

ReNew commissioned a record ~2.4 GW in FY2026 (1.75 GW solar, 0.62 GW wind, 25 MW/100 MWh storage), lifting operating capacity to ~12.6 GW — India's second-largest operating renewable portfolio — with gross capacity at ~20 GW including 1.7 GW of storage, enough clean energy for the equivalent of 24 million households.
