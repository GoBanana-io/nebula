## The idea

Egyptian homebuyers navigated scattered listings, unverified brokers pushing their own inventory, and almost no mortgage infrastructure — high stakes, low trust. Nawy attacked the full funnel: one platform with verified listings across 800+ compounds, its own brokerage arm closing deals, and Nawy Now for mortgage origination — capturing value at every step instead of just lead-gen ads.

## How it works

- Verified multi-listings create the trusted top of funnel.
- In-house brokers close deals rather than auctioning leads to whoever pays.
- Mortgages are originated in-house and passed to banks via securitisation, keeping the balance sheet light.
- Fractional ownership and refurbish-to-rent catch buyers the core funnel can't serve yet.

## Who pays

Commissions, financing spreads, and product fees — three tolls on one journey instead of one toll on three journeys.

## Why it worked

Trust compounds down the funnel: verified supply makes brokerage convert; brokerage volume makes mortgage origination viable; financing makes bigger deals close. Each layer de-risks the next.

## Challenges

Real estate is cyclical and rate-sensitive; holding mortgage risk demands banking-grade underwriting; each MENA market has its own brokerage customs and regulations.

## Steal this

Wherever a big purchase runs on WhatsApp and word-of-mouth, don't build another listings page — build the closing machine: verify, broker, finance. Start with the layer that hurts most locally.

**الفكرة:** لا تبِع الإعلانات للوسطاء — كُن الوسيط والمموّل: قوائم موثقة، وذراع وساطة تُغلق، وتمويل عقاري يُنشأ داخليًا.

**كيف تعمل:** قوائم موثقة تجذب الثقة؛ وسطاء داخليون يُغلقون بدل بيع العملاء المحتملين؛ تمويل يُنشأ ويُمرَّر للبنوك بالتوريق؛ ومنتجات مجاورة (جزئي، تجديد للتأجير) تلتقط من لا يخدمهم القمع الأساسي.

**من يدفع:** عمولات وهوامش تمويل ورسوم منتجات — ثلاث رسوم على رحلة واحدة.

**كيف تسرقها:** حيثما تُشترى السلع الكبيرة عبر واتساب والمعارف، لا تبنِ صفحة إعلانات أخرى — ابنِ آلة الإغلاق: وثّق، وسِّط، وموّل. ابدأ بالطبقة الأكثر ألمًا محليًا.
