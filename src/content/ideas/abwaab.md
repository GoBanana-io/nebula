## The problem

Across the Middle East, a secondary student's chances hinge on which teacher happens to be nearby and whether the family can afford private tutoring — while the free content online rarely matches the country's own national curriculum or its high-stakes exams.

## How it works

Abwaab offers short video micro-lessons mapped to each country's national curriculum, paired with assessments and discussion forums where students can ask questions. The platform covers secondary subjects plus exam preparation, operates across six MENA markets, and — after acquiring Egypt's Eduact — now also supplies SaaS tools that let independent teachers and learning centres manage video content, grading, and payments across online and offline teaching.

## Pain points

Uneven classroom teaching, private tutoring priced beyond most families, a vacuum of curriculum-matched Arabic digital content, and secondary exams that reward memorisation over understanding.

## Business model

Consumer paid learning — lessons, assessments, and exam prep sold to students and families — layered with institutional distribution, and now a B2B SaaS line for teachers and learning centres inherited from Eduact.

## Challenges

Converting free users to paid in price-sensitive markets, keeping content quality consistent across six national curricula, and digesting two Egyptian acquisitions in a single year (Apex Education in January, Eduact in September) without losing focus on the core student product.

## Funding

- Raised: $20M Series A (Nov 2021) led by existing investor BECO Capital with Watar Partners, 4DX Ventures, and GSV Ventures, following a $5M seed round in March 2021 (The Technology Express).
- Earlier: active users grew tenfold during the 2020–21 academic year; the company also acquired Pakistan's Edmatrix to enter South Asia.

## Latest — Sep 2026

On 1 September 2026 Abwaab acquired Egyptian edtech Eduact for an undisclosed amount, adding SaaS infrastructure for independent teachers and learning centres to its student-focused platform — its second acquisition of 2026 after Egypt-based university-admissions startup Apex Education in January (Wamda).
