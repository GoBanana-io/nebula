## The problem

Moving goods across Nigeria runs on phone calls and roadside brokers: manufacturers cannot find dependable trucks, truck owners burn fuel on empty return trips, and drivers — mostly unbanked — wait days or weeks for multinationals to pay.

## How it works

Kobo360's app matches cargo owners with truck owners Uber-style, with real-time tracking from pickup to digital proof of delivery. Drivers receive half or more of the trip fee upfront and the rest on delivery; its Payfasta embedded-finance platform and credit wallets bridge the gap left by slow-paying manufacturers, cutting the collection process by 12 days. The company has described its longer-term ambition as a Global Logistics Operating System combining every activity in the African supply chain.

## Pain points

Ghost trucks and broker markups, no credit history for drivers who lack bank accounts, and cash conversion cycles at big shippers that starve small transporters of working capital.

## Business model

Margin on every load moved through the platform, plus trip-level insurance and financing products and payment flows that monetise the float between shipper payment and driver payout.

## Challenges

The pandemic crushed volumes just as the company chased a $100M Series B; unable to anchor the equity round, it survived on a bridge from existing investors and executive pay cuts before closing a FEDA-led Series B. Unit economics only turned when it stopped absorbing insurance and collection costs and passed them to transporters.

## Funding

- Raised: $20M Series A equity led by Goldman Sachs plus $10M in working-capital financing from Nigerian commercial banks (TechCrunch, Sep 2019), to upgrade the platform and expand beyond Nigeria, Togo, Ghana, and Kenya.
- Later: a Series B led by the Fund for Export Development in Africa (FEDA), Afreximbank's equity arm, with TLcom Capital, IFC, and Goldman Sachs participating (TechCrunch, Sep 2022).
- Valuation: MISSING.

## Latest — August 2023

On 12 August 2023 co-founder and CEO Obi Ozor was sworn in as Enugu State's commissioner of transport, bringing six years of logistics experience into public office — a move widely read as tech talent entering government.
