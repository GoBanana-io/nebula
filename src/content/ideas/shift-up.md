## The problem

Korea dominated mobile and MMOs but had no global console-action success story: investors doubted a Seoul studio could fund, ship, and sustain a premium single-player title against Western and Japanese AAA machines.

## How it works

Shift Up, founded 2013 by CEO Hyung-Tae Kim, runs two tracks. Goddess of Victory: Nikke is a live-ops gacha shooter with constant events and crossovers; Stellar Blade is a story-driven action game that launched on PS5 in April 2024 (a million copies in its first month) and on Steam in June 2025, selling a million PC copies in three days. Nikke's recurring cash flow bankrolls the AAA bets.

## Pain points

Multi-year AAA budgets with binary launch risk, skepticism toward Korean console IP in Western markets, and live-ops churn demanding relentless new content.

## Business model

Upfront premium sales plus recurring gacha and event microtransactions — Stellar Blade contributed 58% of record Q2 2025 quarterly revenue while Nikke keeps the baseline.

## Challenges

Two franchises carry a multi-trillion-won valuation; the next original title must clear a bar Stellar Blade set very high; and public-market investors now expect growth every quarter from a hit-driven business.

## Funding

- Raised: ₩435B (~$320M) KOSPI IPO (Jul 2024) — 7.25M shares at ₩60,000, closing day one at ₩71,000 (+18%), the largest Korean gaming listing in nearly three years (Gaming Amigos). Tencent has been second-largest shareholder since its 20% stake in Dec 2022 (GamesIndustry.biz).
- Valuation: ₩3.48T+ post-IPO, Korea's fourth-largest gaming company behind Krafton, Netmarble, and NCSOFT (Gaming Amigos, Jul 2024).

## Latest — August 2025

Shift Up posted record quarterly revenue on Stellar Blade's PC launch — over a million Steam copies in three days, the fastest-selling Korean title on the platform — with the company's own IR attributing 58% of Q2 revenue to the franchise (Shacknews).
