## The problem

Southeast Asia's startups and SMEs cannot hire fast enough: local tech talent is scarce, Hong Kong and Singapore salaries price out smaller employers, and graduates stare at a job market with no map — who is hiring, for what skills, at what pay.

## How it works

Glints runs a two-sided talent marketplace across Indonesia, Vietnam, Singapore, Malaysia and Taiwan. Professionals join for jobs and career content; employers post roles or hand Glints the whole search. The wedge that scaled it was borderless hiring — matching Hong Kong and Singapore employers with full-stack developers and designers in lower-cost markets — backed by supply-building in new pipelines like the Philippines and proprietary salary/trends data employers actually read.

## Pain points

Engineer searches dragging for months, median Hong Kong tech salaries running 2.5x Southeast Asian levels, SMEs with nobody in-house who knows how to recruit, and early-career candidates guessing at paths from anecdote.

## Business model

Employers pay: job ads, recruitment fees and managed remote-talent contracts, plus career services on the candidate side. The company reports positive contribution margins in every business unit with Indonesia and Vietnam profitable.

## Challenges

Marketplace liquidity must be rebuilt city by city, downturns cut both hiring budgets and cross-border premiums at once, and every market has an incumbent board plus LinkedIn fighting for the same employers.

- Valuation: no citable valuation disclosed.

## Latest — December 2023

Glints' "What to Expect for 2024" talent report found AI-engineer demand up 15% half-on-half with full-stack developers the top cross-border hire, and noted 73% of Southeast Asian startups now hire outside their home markets (TNGlobal) — data that doubles as proof of the borderless-hiring thesis Glints sells.
