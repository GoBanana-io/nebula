## The problem

Korea's retail crypto frenzy ran ahead of its market structure: thin local order books, fragile smaller exchanges, and no trusted default venue where millions of first-time traders could buy and sell safely in won.

## How it works

Dunamu launched Upbit in October 2017 with deep KRW pairs and a fast mobile app, then layered custody, wallets and blockchain infrastructure (including the Giwa chain) around the exchange. It secured Korea's first virtual-asset service provider qualification in 2021, cementing its regulated-leader status.

## Pain points

Illiquid pre-2017 domestic venues, hacks and freezes at smaller exchanges, and retail users navigating unclear compliance and tax treatment on their own.

## Business model

Transaction fees on spot trading volume, with custody, infrastructure and partner services on top — a model that prints money in bull markets and compresses hard in downturns.

## Challenges

Extreme dependence on trading-fee cycles, ever-tightening Korean exchange regulation and listing scrutiny, plus execution risk on the giant Naver merger —including keeping liquidity and users from drifting to Bithumb during integration.

## Funding

- Raised: no public primary raises on record; recent transactions are secondary stakes — Samsung affiliates agreed to buy ~4% (~$408M) and Hana Bank 6.55% (~$670M, May 2026), both at roughly a 15.3 trillion won implied valuation.
- Valuation: $10.27B — Naver Financial's agreed all-stock acquisition (15.13 trillion won, Nov 2025), issuing 2.54 Naver Financial shares per Dunamu share (Reuters).

## Latest — May 2026

Hana Bank agreed to buy a 6.55% stake in Dunamu for about 1 trillion won (~$670M), becoming its fourth-largest shareholder, with joint plans around won-pegged stablecoins and blockchain-based finance — a bank-validation signal six months after Naver Financial's $10.3B all-stock acquisition agreement, one of Asia's largest fintech deals of 2025.
