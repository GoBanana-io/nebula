## The problem

By the late 2010s one platform dominated Iranian ride-hailing, leaving riders and drivers with little alternative — and Iranian startups, cut off from foreign venture capital by sanctions, had no domestic public-market route to raise growth money.

## How it works

Founded in 2016 as Tap30 and rebranded to Tapsi in 2019, the company runs a cross-platform ride-hailing app on iOS, Android, and HarmonyOS across 15 Iranian cities. Services span Classic rides, carpooling, phone-in ordering, parcel delivery, taxi hailing, and rides adapted for disabled passengers, with a fare calculator, driver and ride ratings, and trip-security features built in.

## Pain points

Driver and rider dependence on a single dominant platform, unpredictable informal-taxi pricing, scarce accessible transport options, and thinner coverage outside major cities.

## Business model

Per-trip and per-delivery commissions across its ride, carpool, and parcel services, with growth capital raised on the public market since its 2022 Fara Bourse debut — open only to eligible institutional and professional investors under the exchange's small-company IPO rules.

## Challenges

Snapp's scale advantage forces Tapsi to fight for every share point, including through the courts: Tapsi's complaint to the Competition Council over Snapp's alleged use of fake passenger accounts and scraped driver contacts ended with a ruling against Snapp. In 2023 reports described a breach of Tapsi user data covering about 34 million records, offered for sale via Telegram for $35,000. Operating as a listed company also exposes Tapsi to the volatility of Iran's retail-driven stock market.

## Funding

- Raised: 5% of shares listed in June 2022 — 1.17 billion shares at 9,400 rials each, billing the IPO at 11 trillion rials (about $34 million) on Iran Fara Bourse (Financial Tribune, 15 June 2022).
- Earlier private backing was never officially disclosed in citable English-language sources.

## Latest — June 2022

On 14 June 2022 Tapsi (then still widely known as Tap30) became the first Iranian startup to make a stock-market debut, offering 5% of its shares on Iran Fara Bourse to eligible institutional investors — a milestone the vice-presidency for science and technology hailed as startups entering "the main body of the economics of the country."
