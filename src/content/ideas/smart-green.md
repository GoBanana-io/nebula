## The problem

More than 70% of the world's water consumption goes to agriculture, and Jordan ranks among the most water-scarce nations on earth. Yet the irrigation systems on Jordanian farms are mostly imported, expensive, and built without water-saving features — so farmers flood fields by habit while aquifers fall.

## How it works

Smart Green, launched in 2020 in Jordan and Canada by engineers Mohammad Zeinati and Salma Amayri, builds an Industry 4.0 irrigation stack: Fertigo, a smart irrigation-and-fertigation controller; Rootrika, a root-sensing sensor; and Taqs Scope, a data-based weather app. The system remotely controls the farm's irrigation, delivering precise amounts of water and nutrients to plants exactly when needed.

## Pain points

Imported controllers with no water-saving logic, fertilizer applied by calendar rather than root need, no Arabic-language interfaces for local growers, and no remote management — a farmer had to be standing in the field to know anything.

## Business model

Hardware-plus-software sales: Fertigo units, sensors, and turnkey installations sold to farms and mega-projects, with pilots designed to convert into wider rollouts, monitoring services, and add-ons such as satellite monitoring and water harvesting.

## Challenges

Conservative growers adopt technology only after seeing neighbor pilots succeed, so every new region starts with slow trust-building and awareness sessions. Imported incumbents own existing dealer channels, and regional expansion into markets like Saudi Arabia demands fresh capital, partners, and regulatory groundwork.
