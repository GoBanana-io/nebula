## The problem

Japan's best wind and sun are far from its cities, and when renewables peak the power often has nowhere to go — curtailed at noon, scarce at dusk, while islands and ports keep burning diesel because undersea cables don't pay.

## How it works

PowerX builds the Mega Power 2500: a lithium-iron-phosphate battery block (2,507 kWh) inside a standard 10-foot container that plugs into the grid for supply-demand balancing. Through its Ocean Power Grid affiliate it is co-developing the Power ARK, a ship that carries batteries instead of oil, with Imabari Shipbuilding. The lineup extends to EV Hyperchargers, modular data centers, and storage power plants.

## Pain points

Curtailment of solar and wind, evening peaks the grid can't cover, diesel-dependent remote demand, and developers waiting years for grid connections.

## Business model

Hardware sales of storage systems and chargers, the co-branded "PowerX × Bluestorage" package sold with ITOCHU, power-supply and battery-plant services, and eventually fees for transporting stored energy by sea.

## Challenges

No battery tanker has ever operated commercially — maritime safety rules, insurance, and port handling all have to be invented. Near term, revenue rides on LFP cell prices, METI subsidy windows, and grid-connection queues, while two factories (Okayama, Hokkaido) tie up capital.
