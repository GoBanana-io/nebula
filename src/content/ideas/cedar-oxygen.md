## The problem

Lebanon's post-2019 crisis cut manufacturers off from dollars and bank credit: no import finance for raw materials, no export finance for shipments, and a receivables deficit — a short-term shortfall the fund estimates at roughly $2bn — that threatened factory closures and mass layoffs.

## How it works

Cedar Oxygen, established in May 2020 as an independent Luxembourg-domiciled platform with teams in Beirut and Paris, onboards manufacturers under the patronage of the Association of Lebanese Industrialists, underwrites them on a proprietary digital platform, and extends short-term hard-currency facilities — import finance, export finance and receivables finance — working alongside select Lebanese banks. Its first facility, arranged in December 2020, financed raw-material imports for Lebanese Roasting Group (Castania), a nuts and snacks manufacturer.

## Pain points

Factories unable to buy inputs, shipments stuck without export finance, finished goods unsold for lack of receivables funding, and workforces at risk in an economy with 98% currency devaluation.

## Business model

Interest and fees on short-term structured trade-finance transactions diversified across exporters and non-exporters, plus origination mandates in blended facilities such as the Lebanon Green Investment Facility, where Cedar Oxygen sources Lebanese deals for development and private co-investors.

## Challenges

Converting DFI and endowment interest into the targeted $750M minimum fund size given Lebanon's sovereign risk, maintaining underwriting discipline through volatility, and growing from pilot facilities to a portfolio covering a significant share of Lebanon's import trade volume.

## Funding

- Raised: approximately $1M founder seed, then $175M initial funding from Banque du Liban (Pioneers Post, Jan 2021); targeting a $750M minimum fund size with total capacity above $2bn per year.

## Latest — Feb 2024

On 26 February 2024 UNDP, Lebanon's Ministry of Environment and Cedar Oxygen launched the Lebanon Green Investment Facility (LGIF) — a $50M blended-finance facility, the first of its kind in the Arab States region — with Cedar Oxygen originating Lebanese deals in renewable energy, sustainable agriculture, water conservation and climate-smart infrastructure under CEO Ibrahim Salibi and CIO Mark Smyth (BusinessNews Lebanon).
