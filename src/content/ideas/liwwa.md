## The problem

Small and medium enterprises create most jobs in Jordan and Egypt, yet banks treat them as unlendable: collateral requirements, thin credit files, and slow decisions shut young firms out of growth capital, leaving a persistent SME funding gap.

## How it works

Liwwa runs a technology-enabled lending operation: a business applies online, proprietary credit models underwrite it without traditional collateral, and qualifying borrowers receive loans of up to $100,000 in approximately two days. The loans are financed through Liwwa's debt marketplace at liwwa.com, which sells the investments to hundreds of retail investors across MENA alongside banks and development-finance institutions.

## Pain points

Collateral demands SMEs can't meet, loan decisions that take weeks, founders with no formal credit history, and regional investors with no vehicle for earning returns from SME debt.

## Business model

Originate-and-fund economics: Liwwa earns spread and fee income on SME loans, matching borrower demand with funding from its retail debt marketplace plus dedicated debt facilities from banks and development-finance institutions.

## Challenges

Operating across Jordan and Egypt concentrates currency and credit-cycle risk in the loan book; each growth phase needs new debt facilities while portfolio quality — historically around 2.7% arrears — has to stay intact at larger volumes.

In May 2024 Liwwa secured a $5 million loan agreement with the US International Development Finance Corporation (DFC), aimed at expanding lending to small and medium enterprises in Jordan and supporting job creation.
