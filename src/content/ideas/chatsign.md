## The problem

More than 70 million deaf people worldwide use sign language, yet interpreters remain limited, costly, and inconsistent — so deaf and hard-of-hearing people face barriers at hospitals, government counters, classrooms, and transport hubs. Written text is no fix, because sign languages differ structurally from spoken and written languages.

## How it works

ChatSign's in-house AI model translates speech into sign language performed by an AI avatar, while a camera feed runs through a computer-vision model that transcribes a signer's signs back into speech. Translation runs both directions in real time, aimed at front desks and public spaces rather than one-to-one appointments.

## Pain points

Booking interpreters for every interaction, long waits at service counters, written notices that sign-first users process with difficulty, and institutions that treat accessibility as expensive human staffing instead of built-in infrastructure.

## Business model

No public pricing yet — the team is seeking pilot partners across six target settings (culture and tourism, education, government, front desks, transport, commercial spaces), pointing to institutional licences or deployed devices rather than a consumer app.

## Challenges

Still a pre-commercial spin-out: real-world signing accuracy must be proven in noisy public settings, sign-language data has to be collected with the deaf community at scale, and the patent-protected system must become something budget-holders actually procure. The team is in discussions with potential partners across sectors and continents as it prepares pilot deployments.
