## The problem

In 2011 the Arab world had no Hulu of its own: Arabic films lived on TV schedules or pirate DVDs, and young filmmakers had no affordable way to distribute shorts and features to regional audiences.

## How it works

Cinemoz curates Arab movies, series, documentaries, and shorts — including fresh festival titles from its Dubai International Film Festival beta launch — into a polished free app and website supported by sponsored content and in-video ads, with social features for sharing scenes and snapshots.

## Pain points

Unwatchable-on-demand Arab cinema, piracy, filmmakers stuck between pricey iTunes deals and TV gatekeepers, and interfaces that treated old Egyptian classics as second-class content.

## Business model

Free ad-supported streaming (sponsored content plus in-video ads), extended by licensing its platform technology to MENA TV networks and media agencies.

## Challenges

Licensing costs against thin ad revenue, global giants arriving with bigger Arabic budgets, a product that updates rarely (Play listing at v5.0.1), and converting a free audience into a sustainable business.

## Funding

- Joined Beirut accelerator Seeqnce and secured an initial bank loan for funding within 30 days of joining (Wamda, Dec 2011).
- Monetization from the start was advertising-led: sponsored content and in-video placements rather than subscriptions (Wamda).

## Latest — Oct 2021

Cinemoz remains live and operating: its Android app stands at v5.0.1 (last update October 2021) with 500,000+ downloads and some 100 installs in the trailing 30 days, billing itself as the largest free library of films for and from the Arab World (AppBrain).
