## The problem

Japan runs on small businesses, and small businesses run on paper: invoices read by hand, receipts in envelopes, and month-end closes that eat the owner's weekend. Banks and traditional accountants price for bigger clients, leaving SMEs with software built for someone else.

## How it works

freee's cloud platform lets users photograph or upload invoices, sorts them automatically, and generates financial statements from the scanned data. It plugs into 270+ banks and financial institutions so fund movements reconcile themselves, and layers payroll and HR tools on the same subscription for the mid-market and enterprise plans added since 2017.

## Pain points

Receipt shoeboxes, manual data entry, and paying accountant fees for routine bookkeeping that software can now do.

## Business model

Subscription SaaS across accounting, payroll, and HR tiers, deepened with ancillary financial products sold into the installed SME base.

## Challenges

The IPO prospectus model was explicit — spend to acquire users now, earn it back in fees later — and that trade still shows: H1 FY2026 sales grew 30.8% while operating profit fell 38.8% on growth investment. Money Forward contests every accountant relationship, and moving upmarket means selling against entrenched enterprise ERP.

## Funding

- Raised: pre-IPO venture rounds from corporates including Mitsubishi UFJ, Line, Life Card, Nippon Life, Sharp, Salesforce, Recruit, and SBI, exited at listing (AsiaTechDaily).
- Valuation: above $1B on its December 2019 TSE Mothers debut, Japan's second-biggest IPO of 2019, closing day one up ~35% at ~¥2,700.

## Latest — February 2026

For the half year ended 31 December 2025 freee reported consolidated net sales of ¥19,941 million (+30.8% YoY) with operating profit of ¥531 million (−38.8%) as growth investment weighed on margins, and lifted FY2026 guidance to ¥41,930 million in sales (+26.0%) with adjusted operating profit of ¥2,520 million.
