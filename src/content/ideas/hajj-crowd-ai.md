## The problem

Hajj gathers more than two million pilgrims performing the same rites on the same timetable in a small geography — Mina, Arafat, Muzdalifah, and the Grand Mosque. When density spikes faster than stewards can react, congestion becomes dangerous within minutes, and unauthorised pilgrims add unplanned load to already-full sites.

## How it works

The concept pairs the sensor layer Saudi Arabia already operates — drones, high-resolution cameras, thermal imaging, and the SDAIA-built Baseer and Sawaher computer-vision platforms feeding a Unified Security Operations Center in Makkah — with a predictive layer and a pilgrim-facing app: live density forecasts per route and gate, automatic rerouting suggestions, permit verification, and emergency alerts in pilgrims' own languages.

## Pain points

Crush risk at fixed bottlenecks during the rites; infiltration by pilgrims without permits under strain on the "No Hajj without a permit" enforcement drive; operators detecting blocked routes only after pilgrims pile up; and pilgrims with no trusted real-time guidance on which gate or path to take.

## Business model

Operator SaaS: annual contracts with Hajj missions, camp operators, and transport providers for dashboards and forecasts. Consumer freemium: a free safety-and-navigation app with paid premium tiers (group tracking for families, elderly-care alerts, offline maps).

## Challenges

This is a paper-stage idea: no founders, no product, no regulatory clearance. Any real deployment must partner with the Ministry of Interior and SDAIA, earn pilgrim trust on facial recognition and location tracking, and prove predictions work at Hajj scale — two million people moving as one.
