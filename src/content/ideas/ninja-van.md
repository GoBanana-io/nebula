## The problem

When Southeast Asian e-commerce took off, delivery was the bottleneck: legacy couriers served business districts, addresses were guesswork outside capitals, and sellers needed cash collection and parcel tracking the incumbents never built. Failed deliveries meant angry buyers and lost merchants.

## How it works

Founded in Singapore in 2014, Ninja Van built a courier network from scratch for online retail — pickup scheduling, automated sorting hubs, algorithmic routing, live tracking and COD remittance — and replicated it across six Southeast Asian markets until it was moving 2 million parcels a day. E-commerce sellers get one contract, API integration and dashboards across the region.

## Pain points

Lost and late parcels with no recourse, zero coverage beyond major cities, COD cash stuck with riders, and delivery failures that marketplaces blamed on the seller.

## Business model

Per-parcel fees on domestic and cross-border routes, plus fulfilment, warehousing and COD/returns services — volume economics on millions of daily parcels from e-commerce shippers.

## Challenges

Platform-owned rivals (Shopee Express, Lazada Logistics) undercut independent couriers on price; growth stalled into two layoff rounds in 2024 and a further 12% cut of Singapore staff in August 2025; Vietnam operations were suspended and then exited; and existing backers reportedly led an $80M internal round at about $1B — roughly half the 2021 valuation — with preferential exit terms.

## Funding

- Raised: $578M Series E in 2021 from Alibaba and B Capital Group, lifting valuation to well over $1B and unicorn status (Vulcan Post, Forbes).
- Valuation: well over $1B at Series E (Sep 2021); a reported 2025 internal round discussed ~$1B, about half that peak.

## Latest — Sep 2025

Nin Sing Logistics, Ninja Van's Vietnam operator, announced all B2B and B2C express deliveries would cease on September 30 amid a group restructuring — ending nearly a decade in Vietnam's express market (Vietstock, 8 Sep 2025). The exit followed an August 2025 cut of 12% of Singapore staff framed as a long-term sustainability reset (Vulcan Post).
