## The problem

African tech companies complain of a talent shortage while global firms poach the seniors; meanwhile millions of young Nigerians hold certificates but no practical skills. Universities teach theory, foreign bootcamps cost more than a year's salary, and self-taught learners graduate with no credential an employer recognizes.

## How it works

Founded by Adewale Yusuf, Akintunde Sultan, and Opeyemi Awoyemi, AltSchool Africa runs as an alternative online school: students pay a small application fee (about $24 / ₦10,000), train for 9–12 months in tracks including Software Engineering, Data, Product, Creative Economy, and Business, and receive a diploma certificate. Students without a laptop get one via soft loans, and tuition is paid as a percentage of salary over an agreed period once they earn — a BloomTech-style income-share model adapted for Africa. The school also connects learners to internships and jobs.

## Pain points

Unaffordable upfront bootcamp fees, no laptop, and curricula disconnected from what hiring managers actually test for.

## Business model

Small application fees plus income-linked graduate tuition, device financing, and — increasingly — bulk sponsored scholarships where partners like Binance and Bybit pay full tuition for hundreds of learners at a time.

## Challenges

The promise lives or dies on placement rates in a volatile hiring market; collecting salary-linked tuition across countries is operationally heavy; and each mega-scholarship cohort strains mentoring quality and support.

## Funding

- Raised: $1M pre-seed (February 2022) from Voltron Capital, Nestcoin, Pledges, and Odba VC plus angel investors including musician Ajebutter22, aimed at scaling across the continent.
- Valuation: MISSING.

## Latest — March 2025

AltSchool Africa partnered with Bybit on a $100,000 scholarship fund covering 1,000 young Africans across its diploma programs in software engineering, data analytics, and product management, with mentorship and career counseling included. The deal followed a December 2024 collaboration with Binance funding full-tuition scholarships for 500 learners running January–December 2025.
