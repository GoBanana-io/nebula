## The problem

Asia's appetite for shrimp, crab and lobster keeps growing, but wild stocks are overfished, shrimp farming destroys mangroves, and contamination scandals keep hitting supply chains.

## How it works

Founded in 2018 by stem-cell scientist Sandhya Sriram and Ka Yi Ling, Shiok Meats grew real crustacean meat from cells in bioreactors — showcasing world-first cultivated shrimp, lobster and crab prototypes — and built Singapore R&D facilities while planning a first commercial production plant, also acquiring Gaia Foods for cultivated red meat.

## Pain points

Guilt-laden seafood consumption, fragile supply chains exposed by contamination and disease outbreaks in shrimp farms, and no scalable alternative to wild catch or aquaculture.

## Business model

Premium cultivated seafood for restaurants and retail starting in Singapore (the first market to approve cultivated meat), plus strategic R&D partnerships with Asian food giants and seafood incumbents.

## Challenges

Bioreactor economics stayed far above conventional shrimp prices; the 2022–2023 funding winter starved capital-intensive cultivated players; and with co-founder Sriram exiting at the merger, the Shiok brand survives only inside the combined entity.

## Funding

- Raised: $30M total to date after a July 2021 strategic bridge round with Woowa Brothers, CJ CheilJedang and Vinh Hoan plus existing investors, alongside an Enterprise Singapore Startup SG Tech grant (AgFunderNews).
- Valuation: MISSING.
- Status: acquired — merged into Umami Bioworks, March 2024.

## Latest — March 2024

Singapore cultivated-seafood startups Umami Bioworks and Shiok Meats agreed to merge into a single entity keeping the Umami Bioworks name under CEO Mihir Pershad, with Shiok co-founder and Group CEO Sandhya Sriram exiting after six years and Shiok staff joining Umami's crustacean cell-line work (Green Queen, Mar 2024).
