## The problem

Cracking JEE or NEET from a small town means paying big-city coaching prices or moving cities entirely — a bill most Indian families cannot afford, so talent is filtered by wallet before it is tested by merit.

## How it works

Alakh Pandey began teaching physics free on YouTube; PhysicsWallah (incorporated 2020, now based in Noida) converted that audience into low-priced online courses across 13 categories, then expanded into offline Vidyapeeth centres and hybrid PW Pathshala centres. By June 2025 it counted 4.46 million paid users and 303 offline centres, with nearly 99 million YouTube subscribers feeding the top of the funnel.

## Pain points

Coaching fees that swallow family savings, forced migration to Kota-style hubs, and giant batches where no one answers your doubt.

## Business model

Low-ticket fees at enormous volume across online, offline and hybrid formats, extended by acquisitions in adjacent verticals such as civil-services prep — plus a lending arm (Finz Finance) that can finance its own students.

## Challenges

The company listed while still loss-making (₹243 crore net loss in FY25), offline expansion brings heavy fixed costs and faculty bills, and the stock trades at rich revenue multiples — so any growth stumble gets punished, as the ~21% slide from the listing-day peak showed.

## Funding

- Raised: $100M Series A from WestBridge Capital and GSV Ventures at a $1.1B valuation (June 2022, India's 101st unicorn); followed by a ~₹3,480 crore IPO (₹3,100 crore fresh issue plus ~₹380 crore promoter offer-for-sale) at ₹103–109 per share.
- Valuation: ~$5.2B peak post-listing (Reuters).

## Latest — July 2026

On 17 July 2026 PhysicsWallah's board approved buying an additional 11% of Sarrthi IAS for ₹71.81 crore, lifting its stake in the civil-services coaching platform from 40% to 51% under a multi-tranche deal — deepening its UPSC vertical after the November 2025 listing, when the stock debuted at ₹145 (~33% above the ₹109 issue price).
