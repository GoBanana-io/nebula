## The problem

Cairo commuters lose hours daily to gridlock and informal microbuses — unpredictable, uncomfortable, and hard to plan a life around.

## Why it worked

Swvl put fixed-route buses on an app: book a seat, track the bus, pay cashlessly. Starting in Cairo in 2017, it replicated the playbook across the Gulf, Africa, and beyond, added corporate and intercity lines, and acquired shuttle platforms (Shotl in Spain, Viapool in Argentina) before going public via a SPAC merger on Nasdaq in March 2022 — the Middle East's first $1B+ unicorn listing and Africa's largest US-exchange debut.

## How it works

The original playbook was consumer: book a seat on a fixed-route bus in the app, track it live, pay cashlessly — higher quality than the microbus at a fraction of a taxi. The current playbook is enterprise: companies and government bodies outsource staff and commuter transport to Swvl, which runs dedicated routes and shuttles on its routing software under multi-year B2B and B2G contracts, now spanning Egypt, the GCC, the UK and the US.

## Pain points

Commuters lose hours to gridlock and informal microbuses that are unpredictable, uncomfortable, and impossible to plan around — while employers foot the bill for late, disorganised staff transport.

## Business model

Recurring revenue from multi-year enterprise and government transport-as-a-service contracts, backed by a $13M Sawiris-family and Coefficient-led round plus a $1.5M Sovico placement for working capital; the per-seat consumer business is now the junior partner.

## Challenges

SPAC-era valuations deflated fast; filling every seat profitably on every route is operationally brutal; and the company has since leaned into asset-light SaaS transit software over running its own fleets.

## Latest — August–September 2026

Swvl stacked two in-window financings. On 25 August 2026 it announced a $13M private placement led by the Sawiris family and Coefficient LP to fund its next growth phase and US expansion; on 29 August entARABI reported a further $1.5M definitive agreement with Sovico Holding at $1.46 per share (six-month lockup), taking the strategic round to about $14.5M for working capital and general corporate purposes. Then on 8 September 2026 Swvl announced HSBC had raised its working-capital facility limit by 110% to $1.4M — more than doubling capacity under the line first established in November 2024 — and deepened the tie-up into HSBC global payment solutions. The Egypt-anchored facility funds working-capital cycles behind new and renewing enterprise contracts across Egypt, the GCC, the UK and the US.
