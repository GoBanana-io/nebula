## The problem

Millions of small apparel sellers in Southeast Asia restocked through layers of intermediaries, paid cash up front, and had no affordable way to sell online or ship across borders.

## How it works

Zilingo combined a consumer fashion marketplace with a merchant operating system: sourcing from factories, inventory and catalog tooling, embedded working-capital loans, and cross-border logistics, delivered through the Zilingo AsiaMall and Z-platform apps.

## Pain points

Middleman markups and cash-only wholesale, no credit histories for tiny sellers, prohibitive costs of going online, and cross-border freight that only large exporters could navigate.

## Business model

Take rates on marketplace volume, fees for supply-chain software and services, interest spreads on merchant financing, and margins on managed logistics.

## Why it worked (or didn't)

It didn't. After a $226M Series D (Feb 2019) took total funding to $308M at a near-unicorn valuation, the board suspended CEO Ankiti Bose in 2022 amid alleged irregularities; lenders Varde Partners and Indies Capital recalled ~$40M in venture debt and found buyers for pieces of the asset base. With governance broken and no rescue deal, the board appointed EY as provisional liquidator in January 2023 and the company entered creditors' voluntary winding up — a governance failure atop a real merchant network.

## Challenges

Founder–board trust collapsed completely; recalled venture debt removed any runway for a turnaround; staff and sellers were left with an entity whose liabilities exceeded any recoverable sale value.
