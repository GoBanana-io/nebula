## The problem

By 2023 frontier AI looked like a rich-country club: training a top-tier model cost hundreds of millions of dollars, so only a handful of US labs with giant compute budgets could compete — developers everywhere else were stuck renting closed APIs at whatever price Silicon Valley set.

## How it works

DeepSeek, founded by Liang Wenfeng in 2023 and funded through his quant hedge fund High-Flyer, trains large language and reasoning models and releases their weights openly. Developers can download, customise, and self-host models like R1 and V4 Pro, or call them through DeepSeek's own low-priced API. The January 2025 release of DeepSeek-R1 — a reasoning model built for a fraction of typical US training costs — made global headlines as a serious ChatGPT rival.

## Pain points

Prohibitive training and inference costs, lock-in to closed foreign APIs, and no way to inspect or fine-tune the models their products depend on — the exact frustrations DeepSeek's open weights and cheap tokens address.

## Business model

Volume-priced API and platform access (V4 Pro at about US$1.32 per million input tokens, far above fellow Chinese lab Z.ai's US$0.15 flash pricing but well below US frontier rates), with free open-weight downloads acting as distribution: ubiquity first, paid inference and enterprise adoption second.

## Challenges

US labs keep raising the spend bar; American chip export controls squeeze compute access; open weights are hard to monetise against closed-model competitors; and Washington now treats Chinese open-source momentum as a geopolitical threat, inviting regulatory pressure at home and abroad.
