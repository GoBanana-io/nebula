## The problem

Splitting a purchase into installments in Qatar used to mean interest-bearing credit cards or informal arrangements — a poor fit for shoppers who want to stay within Islamic finance principles. Global BNPL brands never localised for that constraint, and merchants watched carts abandon at the price line.

## How it works

PayLater integrates with merchants online and at point-of-sale so shoppers can spread payments over multiple interest-free installments. PayLater settles the merchant and collects the installments over time, running as a regulated platform under the Qatar Central Bank with a Sharia-compliant model built specifically for the Qatari market.

## Pain points

Big-ticket sticker shock with no halal way to defer payment; checkout pages with no local installment option; merchants losing sales they could close with flexible terms.

## Business model

Merchant-side economics on BNPL volume: retailers integrate PayLater and actively promote it at checkout because it converts browsers and grows basket size — the repeatable merchant retention that, per co-founder Mohammed Al-Delaimi, is what gave seed investors their conviction.

## Challenges

Installment books live and die on underwriting discipline as volumes scale; every merchant must be won against cards, wallets, and rival BNPL; and a maturing QCB licensing framework invites deeper-pocketed regional entrants.
