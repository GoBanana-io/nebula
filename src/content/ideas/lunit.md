## The problem

Breast and lung cancers caught late are far deadlier, but reading every scan needs radiologists the world doesn't have. In developing markets the gap is worst: Thailand and Indonesia lack organized cancer-screening infrastructure, so treatable tumors surface only when it's too late.

## How it works

Lunit's INSIGHT products plug into existing radiology workflows: INSIGHT CXR triages chest X-rays across 10 common findings, INSIGHT MMG (plus DBT for 3D mammography) flags suspicious breast lesions, and SCOPE analyzes tissue-slide images across 15+ cancer types to predict immunotherapy response. The May 2024 Volpara acquisition added precise breast-density measurement and a dataset of 117M+ medical images for building universal screening models. Distribution runs through partners hospitals already trust — GE Healthcare, Fujifilm, Philips, Guardant Health.

## Pain points

Screening backlogs and physician overload, inconsistent early detection across ethnicities and machines, and entire countries where women avoid or cannot access mammography screening.

## Business model

Regulated SaaS: annual licenses per site or per scan, sold direct and embedded in OEM imaging equipment and partner platforms, with Volpara's 2,000-site US footprint as the beachhead for American expansion.

## Challenges

Each product needs its own FDA/CE clearance cycle; AI reimbursement codes lag the technology; and Siemens, Google, and a crowd of imaging-AI startups all chase the same radiology budget.

## Funding

- Raised: $60M venture round (late 2021, pre-IPO); $150M paid-in capital increase (Nov 2023, >100% shareholder participation) earmarked for next-generation products, global talent, overseas subsidiaries, and a strategic CVC arm.
- Deployed: ₩264.7B ($191.3M) to acquire 100% of New Zealand's Volpara Health Technologies (deal signed Dec 2023, closed May 2024; Volpara delisted from the ASX).
- Valuation: ≈$320M market cap at the July 2022 Kosdaq debut.

## Latest — July 2024

CEO Brandon Suh told The Korea Economic Daily that Lunit will push into developing markets after its US/Europe wins — detailed pilot plans for AI breast-screening programs in Southeast Asia and the Middle East, drawn up after talks with Thai and Indonesian officials at Summer Davos, launching within a year.
