## The problem

Obesity is one of the modern age's biggest health problems, yet diet advice stays generic: restrictive plans with no feedback on whether your own body responds. People cannot see the link between eating habits and metabolic health.

## How it works

Avokadio's handheld device samples the breath and measures metabolic gases — ketones, ammonia, hydrogen, methane and other volatile compounds — using nanotechnology sensors. Connected over IoT, the readings flow into Avokadio's AI models, which assess metabolism and return personalised, data-based nutrition advice: a pocket nutritionist starting from a single breath.

## Pain points

Yo-yo dieting, generic meal plans that ignore individual metabolism, and clinical metabolic tests that are expensive and inaccessible.

## Business model

Hardware sale of the breath device with an ongoing subscription for personalised coaching and insights; an early-stage concept incubated at İTÜ Çekirdek, recognised with a 290,000 TL Big Bang Start-up Challenge award.

## Challenges

Sensor readings must survive clinical scrutiny before doctors or dietitians trust them; hardware means tooling, certification, and unit economics far harsher than software; and the team must prove users keep paying after the novelty of the first breath test fades.
