## The problem

International tech giants dominate ride-hailing on Amman's streets, but their pricing, support, and driver terms are set far from Jordan — and drivers working through smart applications pay 30–35% of trip revenue to platforms while lacking any body that protects their rights.

## How it works

Petra Ride pitches global technology with a local touch: riders choose personalised private rides or licensed yellow taxis with upfront prices, live driver tracking, electronic payments, and ratings, while the B2B portal and box/Bekam delivery lines serve corporate staff transport, bulk bookings, and heavy-equipment delivery.

## Pain points

Foreign-designed ride options with no cultural resonance, opaque fares, drivers squeezed by rising platform commissions, and employers and media crews with no homegrown mobility partner for staff movement.

## Business model

Trip-commission economics across ride-hailing, taxi hailing, and deliveries, layered with recurring corporate transport contracts and B2B portal services.

## Challenges

Uber and Careem's scale advantages in drivers and rider habit; a regulator (the Land Transport Regulatory Commission) under pressure over driver protections and unlicensed operators; and the execution risk of carrying the Aqaba smart-transport rollout from agreement to daily operations.

## Funding

- Raised: no public funding disclosed; the company operates commercially through trip revenue and corporate contracts (including Solidarity Insurance staff transport and the Union of Insurance Companies).

## Latest — Oct 2025

In October 2025 the Aqaba Special Economic Zone Authority (ASEZA) signed a cooperation agreement with PetraRide to launch Aqaba's first integrated smart-transportation system — a digital app with driver tracking, arrival times, electronic payments, and ratings — framed explicitly as an implementation step of ASEZA's strategy in line with the Economic Modernization Vision.
