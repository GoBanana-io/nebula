## The problem

Tehran's taxi scene was a maze of official cabs, price-controlled shared savaris, some 80,000 private drivers, and around 200,000 freelance unofficial taxis, with fares settled by haggling. There was no dependable way to hail a vetted car at a known price, especially for young riders in a city of 8 million with dense traffic restrictions.

## How it works

Launched in 2014 as Taxi Yaab by the Iran Internet Group, Snapp works like Uber adapted to a cash society: riders request a driver in the app, the fare is fixed before the trip — "our value proposition; it eliminates haggling," CEO Shahram Shahkar told TechCrunch — and drivers are commissioned only with valid licenses and insurance after background checks and vehicle inspection. From that ride-hailing core it grew into Snapp Group verticals: Snapp Bike, Snapp Box parcels, SnappFood, SnappMarket groceries, SnappTrip travel, SnappRoom, and the Snapp Club loyalty program.

## Pain points

Unpredictable street-taxi pricing, unlicensed drivers, Tehran's traffic-zone rules that punish drivers without local knowledge, and distribution under sanctions — Snapp at one point had to disguise itself as a music app to stay on app stores.

## Business model

Commission on every ride, bike trip, parcel, food order, grocery basket, and travel booking across the Snapp Group superapp, monetising a driver network that reached 1.5 million partners by 2019.

## Challenges

Sanctions reimposed in 2018 forced Western companies out of Iran and cut Iranian apps from the Apple App Store, raising distribution costs permanently. Rival Tapsi's complaint to the Competition Council over alleged fake accounts and driver-data scraping ended in a ruling against Snapp. Scale also brought the usual superapp scrutiny over driver earnings, passenger safety, and user-data protection.

## Funding

- Raised: €20 million Series A in October 2016, led solely by South Africa's MTN Group (TechCrunch). At the time Snapp operated only in Tehran with about 10,000 drivers and half a million users.
- No verified valuation was disclosed (TechCrunch).

## Latest — April 2019

Bloomberg reported Snapp generating two million fares a day — "Snapp Tehran is now larger than any Uber city globally," said co-founder Eyad Alkassar — operating in 34 cities with 1.5 million drivers as Snapp Group pushed into food, grocery delivery, and travel booking.
