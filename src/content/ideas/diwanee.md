## The problem

In 2009 Arab women searching for beauty, motherhood, or fashion content found almost nothing in Arabic — global titles didn't localize and regional publishers clung to print.

## How it works

Founded in Beirut by Delphine Edde and Hervé Cuviliez, Diwanee built three Arabic portals for women — Yasmina for lifestyle and beauty, 3a2ilati for mothers and family, Mooda for accessories e-commerce — with entertainment and social affiliates, run from Beirut, Dubai, and Belgrade.

## Pain points

An Arabic content gap for half the population, advertisers unable to reach women online, and print media failing to make the digital leap.

## Business model

Portal advertising sold against five million monthly readers, e-commerce through Mooda, and post-deal expansion into new products and a print magazine with Webedia's backing.

## Challenges

Soft Arabic display-ad pricing, competition from global platforms and social creators for women's attention, and holding a Beirut-based newsroom together through years of national crisis.

## Funding

- Paris-based Webedia acquired a majority stake in 2014 in a deal widely reported at more than $20M (terms officially undisclosed), among the biggest Arab website deals of its time, plus a further $5M to fund new products (businessnews.com.lb).

## Latest — Jan 2015

On 11 January 2015 Wamda counted the Webedia–Diwanee deal among 2014's six successful MENA exits, alongside Cookpad's $13.5M acquisition of Shahiya — marking Arabic women's content as one of the ecosystem's biggest exit stories (Wamda).
