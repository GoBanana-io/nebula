## The problem

ofo cracked the last-mile problem with dockless rentals: no docks, no kiosks, just yellow bikes unlocked by phone. At its peak it defined what bike-sharing looked like in Chinese cities — and then exported the model to 21 countries.

## How it works

Users put down a 99-yuan deposit, scanned a QR code to unlock a bike, and left it wherever their trip ended. ofo bought, deployed and maintained the fleet itself, scaling from Peking University campuses to hundreds of cities worldwide on venture money.

## Pain points

Riders loved the convenience until they wanted their deposits back; cities were left with mountains of abandoned bikes; and the deposit float that funded expansion became a liability the moment trust broke.

## Business model

Ride fees topped up by the enormous interest-free float of rider deposits — tens of millions of 99-yuan payments that functioned as unsecured loans to the company for as long as nobody asked for them back.

## Why it worked (or didn't)

It didn't. Having raised more than $2 billion, ofo burned through cash buying bikes and entering new markets, and in 2018 it came crashing down: deposit-refund requests turned into a bank run with lines outside ofo offices across China, founder Dai Wei departed mid-crisis, and a bankruptcy application against the company surfaced in April 2019. The rental service collapsed; licences were suspended and assets seized or melted for scrap. From February 2020 the rump firm pivoted to a strange e-commerce app where users could only recover deposits by spending more money inside it.

## Challenges

Growth-first expansion with no path to per-bike profitability; a deposit model that converted a cash crunch into a consumer-protection scandal; leadership churn at the worst moment; and a zombie afterlife that kept promising refunds while withholding them.

## Funding

- Raised: more than $2B by 2018 (Sixth Tone).
- Valuation: estimated $3B at peak (TechNode, Feb 2020).
- Status: closed — bike-rental operation collapsed; millions of deposits never repaid.

## Latest — November 2021

Nothing new to report — the company remains a cautionary tale. The last citable milestone is Sixth Tone's November 2021 report that ofo, three years after its rental service collapsed, still existed as a half-built e-commerce app telling users they could recover deposits only by getting friends to pay the company more money.
